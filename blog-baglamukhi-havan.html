Who is Maa Baglamukhi?

Maa Baglamukhi is the deity who controls speech, thought, and movement of all living beings. Her name comes from "Bagla" (which derives from "Valga" meaning bridle or control) and "Mukhi" meaning face. She is depicted in yellow robes, seated on a yellow lotus in a yellow ocean, holding a club in her right hand to strike enemies, and pulling the tongue of a demon with her left hand — symbolizing the power to paralyze and silence negative forces.

Her primary Siddh Peeth (sacred seat of power) is at Nalkheda, Madhya Pradesh, which is one of the three most powerful Baglamukhi temples in India. Worship at this location amplifies the effects of Havan and puja manifold compared to other locations.

Baglamukhi Havan Vidhi — Step-by-Step Procedure

The Baglamukhi Havan follows a precise and sacred procedure. Under the expert guidance of Pandit Lakhan Sharma, the ritual is conducted with proper Sankalp, Shodashopachara Puja, Havan, and concluding with Aarti and Prasad distribution.

Step 1: Sankalp (Sacred Vow)

The ritual begins with the Sankalp — a formal sacred vow taken by the devotee (or on their behalf by the priest) stating the purpose of the Havan, the devotee's name, gotra (lineage), and the specific blessing sought from Maa Baglamukhi. This personalizes the ritual energy towards the devotee's specific need.

Step 2: Ganesh Puja & Kalash Sthapana

Lord Ganesha is worshipped first to remove obstacles in the ritual. Then a sacred Kalash (copper pot filled with holy water, mango leaves, and coconut) is established, representing the divine presence of Maa Baglamukhi during the ritual.

Step 3: Navgraha Puja

All nine planetary deities are propitiated to ensure cosmic support for the Havan. This step prevents planetary interference and aligns cosmic energies with the devotee's intentions.

Step 4: Maa Baglamukhi Shodashopachara Puja

The sixteen-step worship of Maa Baglamukhi with yellow flowers, turmeric, yellow sweets, chana dal (yellow lentils), yellow cloth, and yellow fruits. All offerings are in yellow (Pitambara) as yellow is the sacred color of Maa Baglamukhi.

Step 5: Havan (Sacred Fire Ritual)

The Havan begins with the lighting of the sacred fire (Agni) and inviting Agni Dev as the medium to carry offerings to Maa Baglamukhi. The priest recites the Baglamukhi Moola Mantra with each Ahuti (offering into fire).

Step 6: Purnahuti & Aarti

The final grand offering (Purnahuti) with coconut, ghee, and fruits marks the completion of the Havan. Followed by the powerful Baglamukhi Aarti and distribution of blessed Prasad.

Sacred Baglamukhi Havan Samagri (Ritual Materials)

The following sacred materials are used in an authentic Baglamukhi Havan:

  • Yellow flowers (Marigold, Yellow Rose, Yellow Jasmine)
  • Chana Dal (Yellow Split Lentils) — primary offering
  • Turmeric powder and raw turmeric (Haldi)
  • Pure Cow Ghee (Desi Ghee)
  • Yellow sweets (Besan Ladoo, Yellow Barfi)
  • Yellow cloth (Pitambara Vastra)
  • Sesame seeds (Til)
  • Camphor (Kapoor) and incense sticks
  • Sacred wood — Aam (Mango), Peepal, or Havan Samagri mix
  • Honey, curd, and raw milk (Panchamrit)
  • Yellow fruits (Banana, Mango, Yellow Apples)
  • Copper Kalash and Havan Kund (fire altar)

The Sacred Baglamukhi Mantra

The primary mantra used during Baglamukhi Havan is:

"ॐ ह्लीं बगलामुखि सर्वदुष्टानां वाचं मुखं पदं स्तम्भय जिह्वां कीलय बुद्धिं विनाशय ह्लीं ॐ स्वाहा"

"Om Hleem Baglamukhi Sarvadushtanam Vacham Mukham Padam Stambhay Jihvam Kilaya Buddhim Vinashay Hleem Om Swaha"

This powerful mantra, recited with each Ahuti, invokes Maa Baglamukhi to paralyze the speech, movement, and intelligence of all evil forces working against the devotee.

How Many Ahutis are Required?

The number of Ahutis (fire offerings) determines the intensity and duration of the Havan:

  • 1,000 Ahutis — Basic Havan for general blessings and protection
  • 10,000 Ahutis — For serious legal cases, business disputes, or persistent enemy problems
  • 1,00,000 Ahutis (Lakh Ahuti) — Full Anushthan-level Havan for severe or long-standing issues
  • 11,00,000 Ahutis — Maha Anushthan for extraordinary situations requiring complete divine intervention

Baglamukhi Havan vs Baglamukhi Anushthan — What's the Difference?

Many devotees confuse Havan with Anushthan. Here's the key difference:

  • Baglamukhi Havan — A one-time fire ritual ranging from 3 hours to 2 days depending on the number of Ahutis. Focused and intense.
  • Baglamukhi Anushthan — An extended ritual practice typically spanning 9 to 41 days, involving daily mantra japa (chanting), daily Havan, and specific austerities. More comprehensive and longer-lasting in effect.

Pandit Lakhan Sharma recommends Havan for immediate relief from urgent problems, while Anushthan is recommended for deep-rooted or long-standing issues.
